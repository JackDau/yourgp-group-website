How YourGP is built

We set out to build services and facilities good enough that GPs want to stay their whole careers. This page explains what that means in practice.

Who we are

A group of GP-led practices in Canberra

YourGP is owned by Dr John Deery and Dr Mel Deery. We run four general practices across Canberra — Crace, Denman Prospect, Lyneham and Garema Place in the city — along with two dedicated services, YourSkin CBR for skin cancer medicine and Canberra Vasectomy.

We have always believed the best patient care happens in GP-led practices. So rather than building a business that manages doctors, we built one that serves them: the systems, teams and spaces that let a GP practise excellent medicine, sustainably, for a very long time.

Everything below follows from that — first what practising here actually looks like for a doctor, then the values, pillars and six questions we hold ourselves to.

For doctors

What's in it for you

If you are a GP working out whether to practise here, start here: how busy you will be, how we bill, the hours, and the colleagues around you. The values and pillars that shape all of it come further down the page, along with what we would ask of you in return.

Will I be busy?

Demand runs ahead of the capacity we have to meet it. We are not asking anyone to build a practice from scratch — we are looking for doctors to absorb patients we already have and cannot see quickly enough.

Our established doctors run at better than 98% utilisation across the year, and the GPs who have joined us most recently filled their books within a week or two.

To put a number on it: Garema Place, our practice in the city, saw about 32,000 appointments across 6,000 patients last financial year out of ten consulting rooms.

How we bill

Private billing, done properly

About 90% of our consultations are privately billed. The rest is bulk billing — mostly rapid immunisation clinics, after-care follow-up and the higher-fee Medicare items.

Patients pay on the day. By the time they arrive they have been told at least twice that there is an expectation to pay, so it very rarely becomes an issue.

The number we would point a doctor to is the DNA rate — patients who do not attend. Every no-show is an unpaid hour of your time, so it tells you more about your day than any headline figure does.

Patients who pay tend to be more engaged in their own care. If someone genuinely falls on hard times we will take the Medicare rebate for a while and resume charging when they are back on their feet. We have standards in the other direction too: if patients will not pay, or will not treat the team with dignity, we move them on. It is rare, and we are slow to do it, but it is an option.

If charging patients feels uncomfortable to you, this will not be the right practice. If it feels fair, you will do well here.

What about the service fee, and what would I take home?

That is the question worth asking, and it deserves a proper answer rather than a headline number. Our information pack sets out the service fee split in full, what our established GPs are billing, what that means in take-home terms, and how we support a new GP while their book fills. Ask us for it and we will send it through.

Hours and leave

Your hours, your leave

You are your own boss. We will tell you what tends to work, and then it is your call.

Your week

We are open 8am to 6pm and you set your own hours within that. We do not work Sundays.

If you want to fill quickly, Mondays and Fridays matter most. We prefer full sessions — a whole morning or a whole afternoon — over school-hours-only, which is tricky to roster around. Saturday mornings run on a semi-regular roster, roughly one a month.

Your pace

You have the option of starting at two to three patients an hour while you settle in and learn our systems.

Most of our established GPs sit at three to four an hour across an eight to nine hour day. That is your call, not ours.

Your leave

You set your own leave. Most of our doctors take three to five weeks a year.

Six weeks is comfortably accommodated. Beyond that we would need a conversation about room utilisation, and we would rather have it openly than have you feel you cannot ask.

Your colleagues

The colleagues around you

You are going to be away sometimes, and what happens to your patients while you are gone determines whether you actually relax.

Each of our practices has an established, experienced GP cohort with a lead GP who knows the patients and the local referral network. They know each other, and they will manage your list properly while you are away.

Across the group we also have GPs holding masters degrees and advanced diplomas in skin medicine, so when something walks in that needs a procedural pair of hands, it does not have to leave us.

Teaching and special interests

Teaching the next generation

We are the preferred training location for GP registrars in Canberra. We host ANU medical students on placement and registrars through the RACGP pathway, and in 2020 we were recognised with an RACGP state award. If teaching is something you want to do, there are supervision opportunities here and we will support you into them.

If skin cancer or minor surgery interests you, our skin doctors are happy to mentor and upskill you, and we can teach and support you to do joint injections. YourSkin CBR shows what that side of things looks like.

How we will succeed

Our Four Pillars

They are sequential. Each one enables the next, which is why we refuse to fix a problem downstream without looking upstream first.

1

Team Excellence

Engaged employees are the foundation. Our reception, nursing and admin teams provide the service that everything else rests on.

2

GP Service Excellence

GPs are tenants receiving service and facilities, not employees being managed. Happy doctors do better medicine.

3

Patient Experience

Patients receive comprehensive, unhurried, evidence-based care — enabled by the two pillars above it.

4

Sustainable Enterprise

Margin protects the mission and funds reinvestment in all the pillars above. Financial discipline is what makes long-term planning possible.

We measure all of it. Staff engagement, GP satisfaction and patient experience are surveyed on a regular cycle, and the numbers are held against clear targets rather than quietly forgotten.

Clarity

Six questions, answered the same way every time

A healthy organisation can answer six questions consistently, at every level. Writing ours down keeps us honest — and tells a doctor exactly what to expect here.

Q1 · Why we exist

Empower great care

To provide the services and facilities we would want to receive ourselves — so our doctors can deliver exceptional patient care.

Q2 · How we behave

Care · Excellence · Kaizen

We tell the truth kindly, we do what we say we will do, and we improve a little every day.

Q3 · What we do

Build the conditions

We build the systems, teams and spaces that let a GP practise world-class medicine — efficiently and sustainably.

Q4 · How we will succeed

Four measured pillars

Team, GP service, patient experience and a sustainable enterprise — each held to a number we publish to ourselves.

Q5 · What matters now

Clear goals

We rally around a small set of clear short- and long-term goals, reviewed regularly so the whole team pulls in the same direction.

Q6 · Who does what

The team serves the doctors

Our reception, nursing and admin teams exist to serve our GPs and their patients. The GP's part is clinical excellence and genuine care.

In return

What we'd ask of you

We would rather put this in writing now than let you find it out in month four.

Clinical excellence, and staying curious

Australian general practice gives you a great deal of autonomy. The doctors who do well here treat that as an obligation to keep learning. Consistently poor clinical decisions are the one thing we would part ways over. It is rare, but it is not negotiable.

Reliability

You turn up, you finish the day, and you tell us when something changes. Patients build relationships with GPs they know will be there, and so does the rest of the team.

Playing as part of the team

Reception and nursing are the foundation of this practice, not support staff. The GPs who do best treat them as colleagues.

Billing consistently with how the practice bills

Around 90% private. This is not about revenue, it is about fairness. If one doctor undercharges, patients start comparing, and the doctors billing properly get treated as the expensive ones.

If most of that reads like a description of how you already work, we should talk. If a couple of them give you pause, better we both find that out now.
